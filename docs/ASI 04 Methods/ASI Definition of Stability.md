# ASI Definition of Stability

> [!abstract] Goal
> Fix what the index measures, so every pillar and indicator can be tested against one definition.

## Why it exists

No UN body defines "stability" itself; the UN's nearby concepts (human security, sustaining peace, resilience) still
define the narrower terms (P45). The owner ruled for Interpeace's tenets (P47).

## Precise objectives

1. Every pillar's umbrella concept follows from an element of the definition.
2. Every indicator can be traced to an element (the plan's alignment table, section 1.2).

## The working definition (from Interpeace's tenets)

> A country is stable to the degree that it holds a positive, inclusive peace: its people have the social, political
> and economic conditions they need for peace. Its state is legitimate and effective rather than merely present: it
> represents all citizens, protects civilians, and provides security and justice to everyone without
> discrimination. Its communities are connected and resilient to shocks. Order kept by coercion alone is not
> stability.
